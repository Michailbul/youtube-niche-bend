# Independent target-niche verification · 5 October 2026

Michael wants **target niches and concrete titles**, with evidence that the
target audience could accept the borrowed format. A winning fitness video is
not evidence that its look will work for women-focused yoga. Read this protocol
before proposing a bend, restyle, merge or new channel direction. It governs
manual and automatic rounds and supersedes older shortcuts that treated an
empty crossing as a proven opportunity.

## Start with a checkable hypothesis

Give the proposer and every verifier the same packet: target niche and content
job, actual video language, proposed title beside the original source title,
thumbnail/structure/visual genes being borrowed, source IDs, intended long-form
duration range, search windows, and our proposed animation treatment. Define
the **exact crossing** as topic + framing/title formula + format + language.
Specify what changes and what stays. A list of appealing niches without titles
is not a deliverable; a title without a target niche is not a verified bend.

For example, distinguish yoga follow-along instruction from an explainer about
yoga. Inspect actual yoga channels before transferring a fitness format. A
demonstration-dependent audience may need movement/body position visible, while
an explanation may accept diagrams. Those are hypotheses until the target
videos are reviewed, not assumptions about women. A channel marketing content
to women does not establish the gender, age or country of its viewers.

## Three scouts, then an independent critical reconciler

The proposer does not certify their own idea. Run these three research briefs
independently, preferably in parallel. Each scout receives the hypothesis and
shared rules, not another scout's verdict. Coordinate paid tools centrally so
the **five-credit cap applies to the whole run**, not each agent. Only the
orchestrator saves final proposals or changes owner records.

| Role | Required investigation | Return |
|---|---|---|
| Topic-demand and language scout | Actual target-topic search results in the proposed language, same month/year windows across candidate niches, relevance exclusions, recent and standing demand, small-channel versus established-channel results, one separate external signal, current/evergreen peg | Raw query log; linked videos with checked views/dates; topic demand and urgency, each with limitations |
| Target-audience and visual-fit scout | Watch/skim target videos, including the opening; compare title/thumbnail, pacing, subject visibility, demonstration needs and recurring visual layouts; inspect the source too | Target sample and channel strips; observed composition/structure versus inferred transfer; Fits / Fits with a change / Untested / Against the evidence, plus specific adaptations |
| Exact-crossing and counterexample scout | Search proposed title and close variants, same topic/formula/format/language; find prior successful and unsuccessful attempts; identify repeated successful competitors separately from low-view copies | Search coverage; successful competitors, failed attempts and near matches; what is actually empty within the searched sample; challenge the proposed opportunity |

After those reports, a **fourth, independent critical reconciler** reviews the
claims and raw evidence. This reviewer is not the proposer and is not merely
the scout repeating their own conclusion. They check relevance, dates,
denominators, sample duplication, competing explanations, source-versus-target
proof, missing visual/timing coverage, and the title's factual promise. Ask them
to find the strongest reason the bend would fail. They issue a final decision
and explicit unresolved checks; the orchestrator then publishes the reconciled
card. Schedule the reviewer after the scouts finish if concurrency is limited.

If the execution environment has no independent-agent capability, record that
limitation. A single agent using four headings is not independent verification.
Keep the candidate Untested; do useful collection work, but do not claim the
independent gate passed. This requirement does not resume a paused automation.

## Target evidence to inspect

- Review **8–12 unique, on-topic long-form target videos from 3–5 channels**.
  Include the actual target genre and language, successful examples and close
  low-view attempts when available. Never pad the sample with adjacent topics.
  Fewer valid videos means incomplete coverage and an Untested candidate.
- For each surveyed channel, show its **top three relevant recent videos**
  within the declared reference window, with thumbnail, original title, view
  count, publication date, checked date and a chronological hover/scrub frame
  sequence. The 8–12-video analytical sample and these channel-context strips
  can overlap; the strips may contain additional videos. Explain omissions.
- Compare source and target frames across the opening and later sections. Keep
  actual timestamps where available. Three automatic middle-video stills are
  fallback evidence, not a full skim or first-30-second verification.
- Say what is visible: layouts, diagrams, cutouts, subject prominence, motion,
  density, pace and structure. Do not infer AI/stock/model provenance. Mark each
  conclusion **Observed**, **Inferred** or **Unknown** and cite the video/frame.
- Established or presenter channels can demonstrate **topic demand, target
  conventions and competition**. Tag that role explicitly. They remain
  ineligible as emerging small-channel proof or our faceless production model;
  their existence does not prove a transferable new-channel opportunity.
- Re-check channel stats today for any source claimed Proven. Source freshness,
  median/repeated hits, first-video age, recent posting and makeability still
  apply; the visual-fit survey does not relax those checks.

## Comparable searches and honest measurements

Log query text, language, topic/framing, duration/content type, period, sort,
locale where used, provider, checked time, result limit/pagination, excluded
videos and failures. Use the same query shapes and month/year windows when
comparing target niches; use native-language equivalents and preserve their
wording. Run plain-topic, topic + history/explained equivalents, source formula
and exact filled-title variants. Record a separate exact-format search where
the wording alone misses the genre.

Report search-result counts as **observed within this coverage**, not total
market size, market share or exhaustive competitor counts. Deduplicate video
IDs; do not count the same hit in two queries as repeated independent demand.
Distinguish lifetime views, average views/day since publication and growth
between two checked snapshots. Views alone cannot identify why an upload failed.

Keep separate readings for topic demand, format spread, exact-crossing
competition, failed attempts and outside-YouTube interest. Do not blend them
into a score. Public views do not prove CTR, retention, viewer demographics or
audience geography. A video language, channel location or thumbnail gender is
not viewer analytics. Those claims remain Unknown without authorized evidence.

## Reconciliation and decision

Source verdict and target verdict are different. A Proven source can lead to a
rejected bend; Format only can supply a packaging gene without validating our
execution. Show conflicts between the scouts rather than averaging them away.

| Evidence state | Decision |
|---|---|
| Relevant demand, successful close target execution and sufficient current coverage support the transfer | A supported candidate; state exactly which crossing is Confirmed and whether it is Contested/Crowded |
| Topic/format demand exists, but the exact crossing or visual transfer has no successful example | **Untested experiment**, even when the searched crossing looks empty; do not label a proven opportunity |
| Missing audience sample, unavailable frames/opening, unverified language/status, or unresolved contradictory findings | **Untested**, with the missing check; no automatic green recommendation |
| Repeated comparable failed attempts, incompatible demonstration needs, insufficient topic demand, or an unsupported title promise | **Reject** the current bend or retain a **Weak** candidate for a specified revision; negative evidence is not opportunity proof |
| Several repeatedly successful exact-crossing competitors | **Contested/Crowded**; require a supported distinction, not merely a new palette |

The private Gallery schema has no Reject enum: do not invent schema fields or
save Reject as a positive proposal. Retain the rejection and its evidence in
existing source/research notes, or use Weak when a concrete revision remains.
An Open reading describes sampled competitive space only; it does not turn an
unverified visual transfer into a validated opportunity. Zero recommendations
is a valid result.

## The final target card

Each proposed target niche gets this checkable card in the existing Gallery:

1. **Target:** niche, content job, language, channel/project association, exact
   proposed title, original source title beside it, and the one-variable swap.
2. **Lineage:** real source IDs/dates/views and the gene supplied by each; show
   source versus target evidence and the original thumbnail.
3. **Audience sample:** 8–12 on-topic video links and 3–5 channel strips with top
   three recent sources. Every count/stat has a checked date and scope.
4. **Demand:** relevant numbers, small versus established-channel distinction,
   separate external signal, exclusions and query log.
5. **Crossing:** exact competitors, successful and failed prior attempts,
   coverage and limits; separate from general topic demand.
6. **Visual fit:** frame-backed observations, inference/unknown labels,
   verdict and adaptation instructions: what to retain, change or remove in
   subject visibility, opening, pacing, layouts and demonstrations. Preserve
   our adopted visual vocabulary; no new style options for unkept ideas.
7. **Package:** title/thumbnail promise, human tension, urgency with a source
   or explicit evergreen basis, episode structure, observed source opening,
   and our separately labelled 0–5 / 5–15 / 15–30-second proposal.
8. **Decision:** each scout's finding, critic's strongest objection, reconciled
   source and target verdicts, disagreements, next falsifiable check, and what
   would cause rejection. Do not claim measured retention for our opening.
