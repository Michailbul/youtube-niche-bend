Niche bend · the method

Niche bend: how Michael decides what to make

This skill carries Michael's decisions as rules. His words behind every rule: references/michael-said.md. What he kept and dropped, and why: references/decisions.md. The tools sit in two working skills:

  • niche-bending-youtube-analyzer: research scripts (scripts/yt_scout.py, format_lab.py) and references (no-go, title psychology, cross-verification, format lab, format families, Danilov method).
  • video-idea-proposals: the proposal board and the feedback history (references/how-we-got-here.md).

The method in one line

We never invent a format. Find a small, fresh, faceless channel whose format took off in the last month, keep the format, swap one variable (subject, universe, country, language). Packaging comes first and comes from other niches. Video second.

The run

  1. Load memory. Read no-go.md, the latest keeps (Gallery youtube-niche-bend, niche-bend/format-lab/keeps.json, KEEPS-NOTE.md) and the last rounds. Exclude anything already kept or dropped.
  2. Find. Pick the entry that matches the request (table below). Discovery: yt_scout.py formulas (title formulas shared across this month), spread (new, established or past peak), search this month sorted by views, formula gaps (every _ explained). Query the Gallery packaging library before YouTube.
  3. Validate. Scorecard per channel with today's numbers (rule 3, 4). Verdict: Proven, Format only or Pass.
  4. Bend. Change one variable. Rank subjects by demand (rule 7). Test the bend on YouTube (rule 6). Run the three scouts and the critic (cross-verification.md).
  5. Package. One format card per find: A thumbnail and title, B its niche, C the format (how it is built, what is on screen), D other niches it fits, each with our title. Source packaging beside ours, tension test, urgency (news peg, live season, evergreen), why the source worked, demand and saturation lines with thumbnails.
  6. Show. A visual board or Gallery cards: thumbnails at feed size, titles, views, dates, many chronological inside frames, the channel's top three recent videos. Lead with two or three ranked decisions and the strongest objection. Then stop: Michael keeps.
  7. After a keep. Design the thumbnail and title inside a YouTube feed mock next to real videos. Only then propose style alternatives. The kept idea goes to production (script, voiceover, visual DNA, scouting, first 60 s).
  8. Write it down. Keeps, drops and his words go into decisions.md, no-go.md and michael-said.md the same day.

What kills an idea

A presenter or real people on screen · a channel over 100K subscribers or one viral hit as proof · a bend with no views last month · a crossing already tried with no views (see no-go) · a description where a famous name should be · a title without tension · a format older than its freshness window · gaming-knowledge or celebrity-footage channels as models · Shorts-only sources.

Formats he pointed at (Warhammer round, 9 Oct)

Labelled grid on a white ground ("The Entire Map of … Explained"), "Your life as …" in drawn characters ("this is what I'm talking about"), the crossover "What if [faction] were in [universe]?", "Every [variant] explained", "Timeline explained", the 3D true-scale reveal, and the 3D timelapse of a place through its history (King's Landing, Middle-earth, Carthage).

Analyzer skill

Niche bending YouTube analyzer

Michael is building narrated 2D animated YouTube channels in any niche that earns it: history, cars, brands, nature, anything with demand. Never assume the target is cars (Michael, 19 Oct 2026: "we dont only want to niche bend into cars!!! this is not always the case"): bend into the source's own niche too, and rank every target by evidence. The rule of the whole operation: reuse what is already proven. We do not guess what people click. We find what they clicked last month, take the part that did the work, and apply it to our subject in our look.

The method started from Tim Danilov's stream "I Found 58 Faceless YouTube Niches In An Hour" (22 Sep 2026). When we re-checked his 44 channels two weeks later, 14 had dropped under 10K a video: one viral video had put them on his list. So we keep his search and bend logic and add our own proof standards.

Frame review preference · 5 October 2026

Michael wants to judge the video by skimming many frames, not by our material-origin labels. Do not infer or write stock-footage, AI-generated or model classifications from appearance. The material source is not relevant to this research. Keep legacy fields for compatibility, but omit productionStyle and source-material styleDescription in new research saves. Use neutral visible composition/format names only when useful. Our proposed animation style, title/thumbnail lineage, structure and hook remain required.

Use the existing Gallery hover/scrub sequence with many chronological samples across the video and its opening. Three fixed stored frames are fallback evidence, not a full skim. Never claim more coverage when richer frames are unavailable. Handoff the live Gallery URL; label localhost explicitly as development.

Target-niche research is independently checked · 5 October 2026

Propose niches with concrete titles, not only source channels or untitled subjects. Every transfer must pass references/cross-verification.md: three independent scouts investigate topic demand/language, target audience/visual fit, and exact-crossing competition/counterexamples; a fourth independent critic then reconciles their evidence and challenges the idea. The proposer cannot approve their own draft. Run the critic in a later wave when concurrency is limited; without independent-agent capability, mark the gate unavailable and keep the candidate Untested.

Inspect 8–12 relevant target long-form videos across 3–5 channels, plus each channel's top three relevant recent videos with current numbers and many chronological frames. Big/presenter channels are eligible only as audience, demand or competition evidence, not as emerging proof or a faceless model. Label findings Observed, Inferred or Unknown. Give frame-backed instructions for what must change in visibility, demonstration, opening, pace and layout. Fitness → women-focused yoga must inspect actual yoga videos; neither audience demographics nor visual transfer follows from the target's name.

Preserve matched topic/formula/format/language and month/year search scopes, queries, exclusions, limits and provider errors. Counts describe the searched sample, not market share. Unknown evidence stays Untested; repeated comparable failures or an unsupported title can reject a bend. An empty crossing is an Untested experiment, not an automatic green recommendation. Public views never establish gender, geography, CTR or retention. Keep the source's proof verdict separate from the target's decision.

Take an outlier apart against the same channel's flops · 19 October 2026

Before crediting a hit's script, structure or thumbnail device, read the transcripts and thumbnails of two or three of the same channel's videos that did not take off. What they share is the channel's house style and cannot explain the hit. Case: The Curious Stick's "Why Attacking a Sleeping Roman Army Was a Death Trap" (2.55M, 612× median). Its cold open, early spoiler, one spoken question and looped ending also appear in its Cannae video (5,162 views), which even had a trap thumbnail ("THE PERFECT TRAP"). Only the hit had a title naming a situation rather than a battle, a reversal, and a thumbnail line that reads like dialogue ("THEY'RE AWAKE"). Worked example and template: 2d-animations/v2/script/TEARDOWN.md. Have an independent critic check the teardown; the first version here scored 6/10.

A format has four genes

Everything below works on these four parts. A proven video is proof for each of them separately, which is what lets us take one and leave the rest.

GeneWhat it isExample
Title formulaThe sentence with a gap that the upload reuses"Everyone Called Him an Idiot. He Got Richer Every Time."
Thumbnail layoutWhat is in frame, where the text sits, where the eye goesA cutaway of a machine, one glowing room, a red arrow, two words
StructureHow the video is builtA tour, a ranked list, a timeline, one question answered
Visual DNAThe look on screenLabelled grid, whiteboard, 2D cartoon, archive collage

Title and thumbnail are the packaging. They decide whether anyone clicks, so they matter most and they are what we borrow first. Structure decides whether people keep watching. Visual DNA is the gene we change most freely, because our look is ours.

The format card: A, B, C, D

Michael's frame for everything we find (4 Oct 2026). For each video that works, record four things:

  • A. Thumbnail and title. The packaging that won the click. The most important part.
  • B. The niche it worked in.
  • C. The format of the video: how it is built, and what is on screen inside (2D animation, mostly static shots, generated stills, 3D, footage).
  • D. Other niches it could be applied to, with the title it would ship with. Only niches that are relevant, popular and already getting views, so the work is not in vain. Not limited to cars.

The four genes above are what a card is made of: A is the title formula and the thumbnail layout, C is the structure and the visual DNA. Running this as a repeatable round is the format lab: references/format-lab.md, scripts/format_lab.py, board at niche-bend/format-lab/.

Look inside every video

A thumbnail is packaging. It is drawn to win the click and often shows nothing of how the video looks. When we checked the frames of the Danilov list, the cutaway on How Ships Work's thumbnail turned out to sit on a video made of real footage, Cowball's white grid on gun footage, and SimpleToon had a presenter on camera in every frame.

So for every video that goes on a board or into the gallery: pull its three in-video frames (yt_scout.py frames), show them next to the thumbnail, and write the description of the look from them. Say plainly when the inside does not match the packaging: then the packaging is still worth taking and the format is not.

Freshness windows

YouTube packaging goes stale fast. A thumbnail style that worked a year ago is now what everyone does.

  • Sources for ideas, angles, titles and thumbnails: at most 1 month old. This is the default of the search tool.
  • Reference channels and videos: at most 6 months old, ideally 2 to 3. Older counts only as a style name ("legacy"), never as proof.
  • Numbers are re-checked on the day. A claim from a video or from the gallery is as old as the day it was saved.
  • When pulling references from the gallery's Videos tab, sort by date and say how old each one is. Anything past 6 months is flagged as legacy on the board.

  • sheet shows the packaging: thumbnail layouts at a glance.

Two ways in

Work out which one Michael is on.

Format first ("find me niches", "what's trending", a channel link). We start from something proven and look for where to apply it. Steps 1 → 2 → 3 → 5.

Idea first ("I want to tell how a brand was built", "a billionaire story", "a video about the Countach"). We start from our subject and go looking for the proven packaging and structure to carry it. Steps 4 → 2 → 3 → 5.

1. Research: find what is proven right now

The goal is small channels with recent videos far above their size. Big channels prove nothing about a format; their audience shows up anyway.

  1. This month, by views. search "<broad word>" --period month --sort views. Skip channels you know are big, collect the small handles.
  2. Title-formula search. Search a title with a gap: every _ explained, the true origin of _, why _ failed. One query finds a format across every subject it has spread to. The list of formulas is in references/format-families.md.
  3. Look at the sheet. Run sheet on the best queries and read the thumbnails. Repeated layouts are formats.
  4. Neighbours. Open a hit in the browser pane and read the suggested column. Formats cluster.
  5. What we have. Check the gallery Videos tab (list_video_refs) and niche-bend/ first so we do not rediscover channels. Apply the freshness windows to what comes back.
  6. Paid sources. If the theartofyt connector is authorised, use it. If not, say so once and carry on.

Drop anything on the no-go list. Run channel on every remaining handle in one call.

2b. Is there room for our version: demand and saturation

We are about to put a proven format on our topic, in our framing. Two questions, and they are different. Demand: do people watch this topic at all. Saturation: is someone already serving it the way we would.

Check three things, in this order:

  1. Topic demand, any format. demand "<our topic in plain search words>". Try two or three wordings, the way a viewer would type them.
  2. Videos over 100K in the last year: the topic has an audience.
  3. Videos over 50K in the last month: it is alive now. Hits a year ago and none this month means it cooled.
  4. Small channels (under 100K subscribers) with hits: the topic carries a video without an audience behind it. This is the signal that matters most for a new channel.
  5. Views all owned by channels with millions of subscribers: demand exists, but it may belong to those channels and not to the topic.
  6. Format demand, any topic. search "<the title formula with a gap>" and the sheet. How many subjects has this format already spread to this month? A format that worked on one channel is one data point. A format that worked on four unrelated subjects travels.

Two more reads that sharpen the call:

  • Supply against demand. Many recent uploads with few views means supply is ahead of demand: saturated even if no single channel dominates. Few uploads with high views each means demand is ahead of supply.
  • Our framing. We are faceless and animated. If every hit on the topic is a presenter in a garage, the topic is proven and the animated version is untested. Say so plainly: it is an open lane and also an unproven one.

2c. Will that audience accept it: audience and style fit

A format is watched by someone. When we move it to another niche, or put another look on it, the question is whether the people who watch that niche will sit through it. Michael's test case: "the entire history of [country]" works as a 2D cartoon in history. Would the same cartoon hold for cars? The way to answer is to look at what the car audience already watches.

  1. Survey the target niche. styles "<niche words>" … --out <dir> collects the niche's strongest on-topic videos of the last year with three inside frames each, on sheets.
  2. Read the sheets and label each video's look in survey.json (drawn, cutouts on a canvas, generated stills, 3D, footage narrated, presenter, chart race…). Mark off-topic ones and drop them. Then tally.
  3. Read the tally with three questions.
  4. Is our look already in use there, and by small channels? A look used only by a 500K channel proves the channel.
  5. If the look is absent: has anyone tried the crossing? Search the bent title. Many attempts with a few hundred views each is evidence against. No attempts at all is an open and unproven lane. Say which.
  6. What does that audience reward from small faceless channels? Those are the looks and angles to carry the idea with.
  7. Check the source channel's own experiments. If it already tried our target niche in the same format, its numbers are the best evidence available.
  8. Think about what the viewer comes for. A car viewer wants to see the car. A history viewer accepts a cartoon general. A format that hides the thing the audience came to look at will lose them, whatever the title promises.

Verdict lines, with the evidence: Fits (the look or a close one already works there on small channels), Untested (absent, no failed attempts), Against the evidence (absent and the crossing has failed repeatedly), Fits with a change (keep the packaging, swap the look or the angle for one that audience already rewards).

On 4 Oct 2026 the car test came out as "fits with a change": no flat cartoon timeline among the 24 strongest car-history videos, a dozen "entire history of [car brand]" attempts under 1K views, while the same audience gave 500K to 900K to a ladder on a white canvas, an inventor story in generated period stills, and a company exposé in illustrated stills, all from channels under 30K subscribers. The round is niche-bend/format-lab/2026-10-04/.

3. Bend, restyle, merge

Three moves. Each one keeps most genes fixed so the proof still applies.

Bend the subject. Keep all four genes, swap what the video is about.

  • Same kind of thing: How Ships Work → planes, trains. Every gun mechanism → every gearbox.
  • Another audience: tennis news → golf. British ancestry → German.
  • Another language: the same videos, dubbed.

Restyle. Keep title formula, thumbnail layout and structure, change the visual DNA. This is how a real-footage or presenter format becomes ours: a garage-presenter "how it works" told as a 2D cutaway, a documentary with interviews told as archive collage. Check that the thumbnail still reads the same at small size in the new look, because the thumbnail is what was proven.

Merge. Build one format from the genes of two proven sources. For example the "Everyone called him an idiot" title on a brand-founder story, with the labelled-grid thumbnail, told as a cartoon timeline. Rules that keep a merge honest:

  • Every gene comes from a source that passed 2a, and the board shows which source gave which gene.
  • Two sources at most. Three is a new invention with no proof.
  • The title and the thumbnail must tell the same promise. A merge that takes a mystery title and a list thumbnail fights itself.
  • The genes must fit the content: a ranked-list structure needs a subject that comes in types, a timeline needs a subject with a history.
  • Name the audience. A merge joins a title from one audience with a look from another, so say who would watch the result and show that this audience already watches something close to each half (2c). If the two halves come from audiences that do not overlap, find a third proof or drop the merge.
  • Look for anyone who already made this merge. If someone has and it worked, it is confirmed. If someone has and it died, find out why before proposing it.

Every bend comes with its title

A bend is never proposed as a bare subject ("planes", "volcanoes"). Michael judges a bend by reading the title it would ship with, so each one is shown as a pair: the source video's title and ours underneath.

Write our title by changing as little of the source title as possible. The source title is the proven object: its word order, length, capitals, punctuation, numbers and emotional word did the work. Swap the subject and leave everything else alone.

  • Source: "Every Gun With A Strange Mechanism Explained" → ours: "Every Car With A Strange Mechanism Explained"
  • Source: "The Flight Hitler Wasn't Supposed to Survive" → ours: "The Race Niki Lauda Wasn't Supposed to Survive"
  • Source: "The True Origin of the Spanish Civil War: What Historians Get Wrong" → ours: "The True Origin of the Porsche 911: What Enthusiasts Get Wrong"

The tension test (Michael, 4 Oct 2026). A title works when it sets two things the viewer already knows and cares about against each other. "The World Champion Formula 1 Rejected" works because both sides are known and they contradict: a champion, and rejected. "The Mechanic Toyota Rejected" fails: "mechanic" could be anyone, and Toyota means nothing to the viewer here, so there is no tension and no open loop. Before proposing any title, check:

  • Every noun that carries the title is recognisable to a general viewer: a famous name (Ferrari, Honda, Lamborghini), a status everyone understands (world champion, richest man), or a thing they have seen (the Beetle, the SUV). A job title or a description standing in for a name ("the mechanic", "a loom maker's son", "the engineer") is dead weight. Use the famous name.
  • The two sides pull against each other: success despite something, a rejection of someone who then won, a thing everyone mocked that outsold everything.
  • It is human. People click to see someone succeed against something, or to see how people differ. An adjective that names a human quality ("fearless", "delusional", "greedy") does work; a neutral descriptor does not.
  • It has urgency (Michael, 4 Oct 2026). "Porsche Lost 93% of Its Profit — Here's WHY" works because it is news: the viewer needs to know now. The same shape on old history ("Lamborghini went bankrupt and changed hands five times") has no reason to be clicked today. Give every idea an urgency line: a live news peg, a season in progress, a current product, or say plainly that it is evergreen. A history video can borrow urgency by opening on this month's news and telling the history as the answer.
  • Words do work. "How a Fearless Engineer Built…" gets clicked; "How a Loom Maker's Son Built…" triggers nothing. The sourced rules and the word lists are in references/title-psychology.md (4 Oct 2026): read it before writing titles.
  • If the swap removes the recognised name or the contradiction, the bend does not fit this formula. Say so and drop it.

When you write demand and saturation on a board, show the thumbnails of the videos you are talking about, each linked to the video.

How to do it well:

  • Start from the source channel's best title of the last month or two, the one with the most views over its median. A different bend can use a different source title from the same channel if it fits better.
  • Change a second word only when the subject forces it ("Historians" → "Enthusiasts"). If you find yourself rewriting the sentence, the bend does not fit this format: say so and drop it.
  • The title must be true. If the formula needs a fact you have not checked (a name, a number, an event), put it in [brackets] and never invent one. A title the video cannot pay off loses the viewer in the first minute.
  • A merge follows the same rule with the title gene's source. A restyle keeps the title unchanged.
  • On the board, the pair is one clickable unit: bend name, our title, then "from:" and the source title.

Reuse a served channel's format on a new idea (Michael, 4 Oct 2026). When a channel inside our own niche already serves an audience (Rare Car Storys with single-engine mysteries, The Sleeper List with car-industry news), we do not copy its subjects. We take its format, visual language or thumbnail layout, which that audience already clicks, and put an idea or a title from another niche on it. On a board this is a merge, and it is labelled as one.

Style options come after a winner. Ideas and titles are proposed first. Alternative visual styles for a video are proposed only for an idea Michael has kept, never for the whole list.

For every bend, restyle or merge, run 2b before showing it. Offer three per source and say which gene changed in each.

4. Idea first: find the angle and the packaging

Michael has a subject. We need the angle (what the video promises) and the packaging to carry it, taken from niches that have nothing to do with the subject.

  1. Check the packaging library first: youtube-niche-bend in the gallery already holds proven titles and thumbnails with their formulas.
  2. Name what kind of story it is, in types that exist in every niche. A brand story is usually one of: an origin, a rivalry, a rise against the odds, a collapse, a hidden truth, a single decision, "how it works inside". A person's story: the outsider everyone doubted, the feud, the fall, the secret. Write down the two or three that fit the facts.
  3. Hunt each story type across other niches, this month. Search title formulas that carry that type with the subject left as a gap: everyone called him _, the _ that destroyed _, how _ lost everything, why _ failed, the true origin of _, the man who _. Run sheet on each. We are looking for outliers anywhere: history, science, sports, food.
  4. Collect 8 to 12 outlier packages. For each: the title, the thumbnail, views, channel size, age, and one line on why the click happens (the gap between what the viewer expects and what the title claims).
  5. Transplant. Reduce each title to its formula and fill it with our subject. Describe the thumbnail as a layout (what is in frame, the two or three words, where the eye goes) and redraw that layout with our subject in our visual DNA. Keep the source next to our version.
  6. Check the facts carry it. A formula that needs a villain needs a real villain in our story. If the facts do not support the promise, drop that package. Clickbait that the video cannot pay off kills retention.
  7. Validate with 2b, then order by strength of proof.

B. Checklist: 15 rules

  1. The viewer must already know something about the subject, because curiosity is the wish to close a gap in what you know and no gap opens around a thing you have never heard of. George Loewenstein, "The Psychology of Curiosity", 1994, as summarised in Kidd & Hayden, Neuron 2015 (snippet).
  2. Hang the title on a name that is already in the viewer's head: Paddy Galloway's example is "the UN is holding back the Sahara Desert", which works because the UN is "super well known" and "top of mind". Creator Science, Paddy Galloway episode (read).
  3. "Be familiar, but unexpected": a known subject or format, plus one thing that contradicts what the viewer assumes about it. Paddy Galloway in Colin and Samir, The New Rules of YouTube (read).
  4. Pair curiosity with fear or with desire: in Jake Thomas's set of 408 outlier titles, 61% carry curiosity, 46% desire and 40% fear, and the usual winner is curiosity plus one of the other two. Creator Science #125, Jake Thomas (read).
  5. Negative words raise clicks: across about 105,000 headline variants and 370 million impressions on Upworthy, each added negative word lifted click-through by 2.3%, and positive words lowered it. Robertson et al., Nature Human Behaviour, 2023 (snippet; news headlines, the nearest controlled data we have for titles).
  6. Pick the most extreme true verb: MrBeast's handbook says "I Survived" beats "I Spent" and "in general, the more extreme, the better". "How to Succeed in MrBeast Production", quoted by Cybernews (snippet).
  7. Use a noun the viewer can picture: "I Spent 50 Hours In My Front Yard is lame… but you would click I Spent 50 Hours In Ketchup". MrBeast handbook, in Simon Willison's notes (read).
  8. Superlatives, versus match-ups and three-step comparisons are the three structures Galloway names as steady performers ("World's Fastest Drone vs F1 Car", "$25K vs $25M"). Colin and Samir (read); Aprilynne Alter says the same of adjectives, "especially superlatives", in Creator Science #196 (read).
  9. A status label everyone understands does the work of a famous name: "Harvard negotiator explains how to argue" scored +553 on Creator Hooks. Creator Hooks (read).
  10. One idea per title: "the more commas and the more ands you put in your title… it's just not going to have a focus". Jake Thomas, Creator Science #125 (read).
  11. Stay under about 55 characters and put the hook word first, because the mobile feed cuts titles near 40. Jake Thomas, same episode (read); the 40 and 60 cut points are from yt-package (read, unsourced there).
  12. Aim between too narrow and too vague: "Nikon B500 review" loses reach, "my favourite camera ever" targets nobody, "best camera for vlogging" works. Jake Thomas on Creators on Air (read).
  13. The idea has to be understood in under a second, and if it cannot be put into a title and thumbnail it is no longer an idea. Paddy Galloway, Creator Science (read) and his five traits thread (snippet).
  14. Take the formula from an outlier in another niche: Thomas's "Do this and you will never hit a fat iron shot" (golf) and "Do this to get them hooked in the early stages of dating" were both outliers on their channels. Net Influencer interview (read).
  15. The first minute must show that the title's promise will be kept, since the title and thumbnail set the expectation the whole video is measured against. MrBeast handbook, in Simon Willison's notes (read); Galloway calls it "overpromise and overdeliver" in the thread above.

For the thumbnail that sits beside the title: at most three things for the eye (Galloway, glance test), and in vidIQ's study of 500 breakout videos from June 2026, 89% used a face or high contrast and the median text was five words (vidIQ, snippet). We have no face, so contrast and a famous object have to carry it.

What makes a subject "recognisable enough" (our reading)

No practitioner gives a threshold. Putting rules 1, 2, 9 and 12 together with Michael's feedback, a title noun passes when a general viewer can do one of three things without help:

  • Name it: Ferrari, Formula 1, Porsche, the Beetle, Henry Ford.
  • Rank it: world champion, richest man, world's first, fastest, biggest. The status is known even when the person is not.
  • Picture it: a tractor, a taxi, a tank, ketchup.

A noun that needs a sentence of explanation fails (a supplier brand, a model code, an engineer's surname outside car circles). Then check the pairing: two recognised things that should not go together make the tension ("champion" and "rejected"). One recognised thing plus one unknown thing makes a label.

Michael's two examples, explained with the rules

  • "The World Champion Formula 1 Rejected": a status everyone ranks (rule 9), a name everyone knows (rule 2), a negative verb (rule 5), and the two contradict (rule 3). Short, one idea (rules 10, 11).
  • "The Mechanic Toyota Rejected": "mechanic" has no rank and no face, so rule 1 fails and there is nothing to contradict. Toyota is known, but the viewer has no expectation about whom Toyota hires, so "rejected" breaks nothing.
  • "How a Fearless Engineer Built the World's First Car": a superlative that everyone ranks (rule 8), an object everyone pictures (rule 7), and an emotional adjective in place of a biography.
  • "How a Loom Maker's Son Built…": "loom maker's son" is a fact the viewer has no prior knowledge of (rule 1) and it is too narrow (rule 12). It would work only after the video has taught them who he is.

What kills a title, in one list

  1. A carrying noun the viewer cannot name, rank or picture.
  2. Two known things with no contradiction between them.
  3. A promise the first minute does not pay (rule 15).
  4. Two ideas in one title (rule 10).
  5. The hook word after character 40 (rule 11).
  6. Thumbnail text that repeats the title's words. Every skill in section A agrees on this, and none gives a source.

Cross-verification

Independent target-niche verification · 5 October 2026

Michael wants target niches and concrete titles, with evidence that the target audience could accept the borrowed format. A winning fitness video is not evidence that its look will work for women-focused yoga. Read this protocol before proposing a bend, restyle, merge or new channel direction. It governs manual and automatic rounds and supersedes older shortcuts that treated an empty crossing as a proven opportunity.

Start with a checkable hypothesis

Give the proposer and every verifier the same packet: target niche and content job, actual video language, proposed title beside the original source title, thumbnail/structure/visual genes being borrowed, source IDs, intended long-form duration range, search windows, and our proposed animation treatment. Define the exact crossing as topic + framing/title formula + format + language. Specify what changes and what stays. A list of appealing niches without titles is not a deliverable; a title without a target niche is not a verified bend.

For example, distinguish yoga follow-along instruction from an explainer about yoga. Inspect actual yoga channels before transferring a fitness format. A demonstration-dependent audience may need movement/body position visible, while an explanation may accept diagrams. Those are hypotheses until the target videos are reviewed, not assumptions about women. A channel marketing content to women does not establish the gender, age or country of its viewers.

Three scouts, then an independent critical reconciler

The proposer does not certify their own idea. Run these three research briefs independently, preferably in parallel. Each scout receives the hypothesis and shared rules, not another scout's verdict. Coordinate paid tools centrally so the five-credit cap applies to the whole run, not each agent. Only the orchestrator saves final proposals or changes owner records.

  1. Watch for formats that are new and spreading (Michael, 4 Oct 2026). We want to catch it while it is still crossing, and be the one who brings it to our niche.
  2. yt_scout.py formulas --out <round>/formulas.json --table sweeps this month's uploads over 100K across the niches and title shapes in scripts/watch_seeds.json, and lists title wording shared by three or more channels, with the niches each was seen in.
  3. Read the list and drop what is a phrase and no format ("N years ago"), a news event, a language tag, or one collab counted under several channel names.
  4. Run yt_scout.py spread "<formula with _ for the gap>" on each survivor. It compares the last month with the rest of the year:
  5. New and spreading: 70% or more of the formula's views came in the last month, with 3 or more videos over 100K. This is the one to move on. Example on 4 Oct 2026: "Engineers Tore Down a … What They Found", 13 videos over 100K on 24 channels, 98% of its views inside the month.
  6. Established and still working: it had views before this month and still gets them ("… Is Worse Than You Think": 18 over 100K this month, 22 in the year). Safe, and no longer a head start.
  7. Past its peak: the views came earlier in the year and this month is mostly copies ("The 7 Levels of …": 27 over 100K in the year, 4 this month from 18 channels).
  8. Thin: fewer than 3 hits this month.
  9. For each new or established formula, note which niches it has reached and whether ours is among them. A formula that has crossed two or more niches and has not reached ours is the best find. One that started inside our niche (the teardown) is already served there: take it to another niche or leave it.
  10. These formulas become the sources of this round's cards. Everything below still applies to them.
  11. Collect what worked. Sources, in this order: the gallery's packaging library (youtube-niche-bend), this month's searches (yt_scout.py search with title formulas and broad words, --period month), a niche survey (yt_scout.py styles), and anything Michael sends. Keep videos under a month old where you can; note the age when older.
  12. Pick 6 to 8 sources. A source qualifies when it is an outlier (5× its channel's median or its subscriber count), the channel is not big (under 100K), and it is not on the no-go list. Run yt_scout.py channel on each: a cooled channel or a one-hit channel still gives packaging, and the card says so.
  13. Write A, B, C for each from the thumbnail, the channel and the frames.
  14. Propose D: three target niches per format, each with a title that changes as little of the source title as possible. Before proposing, look at what the source channel already tried: its own weak videos are the cheapest evidence there is (Odd Machines made "Weirdest Cars Ever Made Explained" in the same format and got 4.6K against 1.2M for combat robots).

Run it twice: once on the plain topic (the whole pie) and once on the wording of our kind of video ("f1 history|f1 story"), because the pie is mostly official highlights and the slice is where we compete. Read three numbers:
  16. Size: top-20 views this month and Wikipedia views per day. On 4 Oct 2026 Formula 1 was about 7 times rally on both (85M against 12.5M; 5K a day against 757).
  17. Ceiling for a small channel: the best and the third-best video from channels under 100K subscribers, in the story slice. F1: 986K and 373K. Rally: 268K and 114K. This is the realistic upside for us, and the number to rank targets by.
  18. Now or always: a Wikipedia trend above 1.3, a season in progress or a news peg means now. Otherwise it is evergreen, and say so. Prefer the bigger topic when its crossing is open. A small topic is worth proposing only when small channels take a large share of it or the crossing is confirmed. Put the size line on the card next to the verdict.

Danilov method

Tim Danilov's method

Source: "I Found 58 Faceless YouTube Niches In An Hour", Tim Danilov Biz, 22 Sep 2026, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Lo_JRTjq3fc. Transcript: niche-bend/tim-danilov-58-niches/transcript.txt. He runs faceless channels (The Art of War, Wholesome Wendy, Zeck Felms) and sells a coaching program, so read his income numbers as marketing. The search and bend logic is what we keep.

His six checks for a niche

  1. Launch budget under $1,000 a month. Above that, the money at risk on something unproven grows too fast.
  2. Can realistically reach $15K a month within three months. Otherwise he does not enter.
  3. The niche started less than six months ago. "If the niche is older than six months, there's no potential for you to enter it. Typically those niches are already taken."
  4. Fewer than three competitors, so it can be dominated.
  5. You can come up with a few bends for it.
  6. You would enjoy running it.

Where we differ: we add a 50K median floor and a one-hit test (he judges by the most popular video), and we require faceless with no AI presenter (he rates AI presenters highest: "AI influencers are the highest rank possible").

What he reads on a channel page, in about 20 seconds

  • Views against subscribers. "The more views they get and the less followers they have, the better for you. It means the channel is fresh."
  • Videos tab → Popular, then the age of the top video. He wants "two weeks ago", "a month ago".
  • Videos tab → Oldest. When did it start. Four months is "perfect". A year is a reject.
  • Is it still posting. A channel that stopped "probably got demonetized". Skip.
  • How many uploads. Many uploads with a low average scores lower. Tennis Shadow Report got a 3 for that.
  • Collab feature. A new channel fed with traffic from a sister channel is inflated. Note it.
  • How it is made. He guesses the tool (Vidrush, AI stills, whiteboard, real animation) to price it. AI stills: cheap and "99% margin" but risky. Hand animation: "for whoever has $1,000 a month to spend on animators".

His search methods

  1. A curated database (his paid dashboard). Not available to us unless the theartofyt connector is authorised.
  2. A Claude connector that queries YouTube per market. He asked for history, fitness, news and politics, fishing, true crime, psychology, relationships, celebrities, one report each.
  3. Incognito search, free:
  4. Logged out, search a broad word, filter to This month, sort by view count. Open small channels.
  5. Search a title formula with a gap: "every _ explained". "This allows you to find all of the videos that used that particular formula."
  6. After clicking several videos of one kind, return to the home page: it now recommends more of that kind.
  7. Community: Discord alerts, a friend. People who watch YouTube for a living pass niches to each other.

Target niches and independent review · 5 October 2026

Read the analyzer's references/cross-verification.md for every new proposal. A source hit establishes a source opportunity; it does not establish that the same style or title will work in a new target niche. Propose both a target niche and an actual title, not just a source channel or a broad topic. Name the target language and narrower viewer intent, distinguishing observed targeting from inferred audience characteristics.

Each surviving crossing needs three independent scouts: (1) topic demand and urgency, (2) target-video visual conventions and transferable style, (3) exact-crossing competition and earlier attempts. Scouts receive the hypothesis and shared scope, not a predetermined green verdict.

Where sources come from

  • Targets are any niche, not only cars (Michael, 19 Oct 2026). A bend can stay in the source's niche with a new subject (history → history) when demand supports it, or go anywhere else. Rank by evidence.
  • Ideas, titles and thumbnails come from other niches. Prehistory, space, weapons, football, nature, business, war history. We do not follow what is trending among car channels, because that audience is already being served that way.

The title tests

A proposed title must pass all five. These came from Michael's rejections; the cases are in how-we-got-here.md.

  1. Recognisable on both sides. Every noun that carries the title is something a general viewer can name (Ferrari, the Beetle, Formula 1), rank (world champion, world's first, biggest) or picture (an SUV, a tractor). A job or a description standing in for a name is dead weight: "the mechanic", "a loom maker's son", "the engineer". Use the famous name.
  2. Tension. The two sides contradict: a champion, yet rejected; called ugly, yet outsold everything; the most loved team, and it cheated. If you cannot say the contradiction in one line, there is none. Write that line on the board as Tension.
  3. Human. People click to watch someone succeed in spite of something, or to see how people differ. An adjective that names a human quality does work ("fearless", "delusional", "greedy"); a neutral descriptor does not.
  4. Urgency. A news title works because the viewer needs to know now. The same shape on old history has no reason to be clicked today. Every idea states its urgency: a live news peg, a season in progress, a current product, or "evergreen" said plainly. A history video can borrow urgency by opening on this month's news and telling the history as the answer ("Porsche Lost 93% of Its Profit — It Has Been Here Before").
  5. Close to the source. Swap the subject and leave the word order, length, capitals, punctuation and numbers alone. If the subject forces you to rewrite the sentence, the bend does not fit this formula. A fact not yet checked goes in [brackets]; never invent one.

When a source title works, say why in the terms above before bending it. "The World Champion Formula 1 Rejected" works because of a status everyone understands set against a rejection; a bend has to keep both, and "The Mechanic Toyota Rejected" kept neither.

Choose the subject by demand: the subject ladder

A format can be filled with many subjects from our niche, and they are not equal: Formula 1 has several times the audience of rally. Before writing bends, rank the candidate subjects, and fill each proven format with the highest subject that fits its facts (Michael, 4 Oct 2026).

How to build the ladder:

  1. List the sub-topics of the niche (for cars: Formula 1, rally, Le Mans, NASCAR, each big brand, engines, design, muscle cars, JDM, EVs, Chinese cars).
  2. Run yt_scout.py demand "<sub-topic> history" and "<sub-topic> explained" for each, the same two wordings for all so the numbers compare.
  3. Per sub-topic, pool both results and record two readings: standing demand (median of the ten best videos within the year, and how many passed 100K) and heat (the same for this month).
  4. Read the top results of each and discount what is off-topic ("rally" pulls tennis and games; a brand name pulls dealer listings).

How to use it:

  • Standing demand ranks the subjects. Heat says what is trending now and supplies the urgency line.
  • The month reading is noisy, because YouTube's search returns only a few dozen videos. Trust the year median for ranking and treat the month as a signal only when several videos agree.
  • This is a relative ranking from YouTube search results. It is not search volume, and it is YouTube only. Demand has to be confirmed outside YouTube too (Michael, 4 Oct 2026). Cross-check the ladder against at least one of:
  • Google Trends, through the browser pane: https://trends.google.com/trends/explore?date=today%2012-m&q=a,b,c,d,e&hl=en (five terms at most; add &gprop=youtube for YouTube search interest). Read the "Average" row with get_page_text. Keep one anchor term (e.g. ferrari) in every group so groups compare. Go slowly: several loads in a row and the charts come back empty. Watch for words with other meanings ("rally" is also political).
  • Wikipedia page views: scripts/wiki_views.py "Label=Article_title" …. Free and stable, good for general interest in a name.
  • vidIQ (official connector, https://mcp.vidiq.com/mcp, works on the free plan and spends vidIQ credits): vidiq_keyword_research gives search volume and competition per keyword. Use it when it is connected; if it is not, say so once.
  • The theartofyt connector, when authorised.
  • When the sources disagree, show all of them and say which you trust for this question. Report them as separate columns, never one blended score.
  • Show the ladder on the board, above the ideas, and put each idea's subject rung on its row.
  • A high rung does not rescue a title that fails the tests. A strong story in a lower-demand topic may justify a clearly labelled experiment, but an empty crossing alone supplies no success proof. Apply the independent evidence gate before recommending it.

Video production

Video production phases

Michael's shared workflow, set 8 October 2026. Read this when a kept idea enters production for our narrated YouTube films. Individual film status, versions and implementation notes belong in that film's own documentation. Focused revisions to approved work do not restart the whole sequence.

Cohesion applies at every phase

Michael's standard is 100% alignment between what we hear and what we see, with the cohesion of premium YouTube creator work. Narration, footage, graphics, motion and sound must communicate the same story moment and feel designed together. Subjects, model identities, facts, numbers, places and time must agree. Visual reveals must land with the relevant spoken words, and music and effects must support their meaning and emotional delivery.

Use the project's established visual DNA and kept premium creator references to judge typography, image treatments, hierarchy, motion, transitions and the mix. Graphics should clarify or demonstrate the narration. All parts must fit the same direction; resolve style drift and generic filler during review.

Critic agents explicitly check each narration beat against its actual footage, graphics and audio timing. Record mismatches against beat IDs or timecodes and fix them before the work is presented for approval. Missing files, uninspected clips and unplayed audio are incomplete evidence; do not certify alignment without reviewing them. Review the first-minute V1 and later cuts with picture and sound together.

SCRIPT

Start from the kept idea and its packaging. Analyse the competitor/source video and the openings of comparable videos that performed well: the promise, tension, reveal, beat order and payoff. Inspect their actual openings and record the source links, relevant timestamps and checked performance; public views do not establish retention or explain why a hook worked.

Research the subject and check the claims. Bend the proven story structure and hook mechanism into an original script for our subject and audience, preserving source lineage. Write our own words from checked research. A source transcript is evidence, not text to copy.

Always use a separate critic agent, with .claude/agents/film-critic.md as the project review brief. Give it the actual draft, source/hook evidence and fact notes; revise the script until it reaches at least 9/10. Show Michael the complete script and obtain his explicit approval of that version before generating voiceover. A critic score and an approved title are not script approval. If wording changes after approval, get the changed script approved again.

VOICEOVER

Generate narration from the exact approved script. Keep the script version, voice settings and takes together in the film's documentation. Have the independent critic listen to the actual audio and check fidelity to the script, pronunciation, emotional delivery, natural pacing and the opening performance. Revise the take until it reaches at least 9/10 before moving into visual production. A transcript or waveform alone cannot certify the vocal performance; if listening is unavailable, record an incomplete review.

Use the accepted narration as the clock for the boardmatic and edit. Preserve its timings and map the story beats to the spoken words.

VISUAL DNA

Check the project's existing art direction, kept references and reusable technique packs first.

Use the connected Laniameda Gallery as the starting point for visual inspiration and animation recipes. Verify check_connection returns ok: true and authenticated: true, followed by a successful read. Resolve the current YOUTUBE CARS collection and its Explainer Styles & Skills shelf through list_collections; browse with list_skills, and read fitting packs in full with get_skill. Inspect their bodyMedia and step frames/clips. Browse Posters & Motion Graphics and the other current YouTube Cars shelves with asset listings and visual previews. The old YouTube Docs folder is a legacy location; follow the live filing. If semantic search fails, use collection and keyword listings and disclose the limitation rather than treating it as lost Gallery access.

  • Visual DNA already exists: reuse it and go directly to SCOUTING. Do not invent a new direction or repeat the art-direction phase.
  • Visual DNA is missing: gather references, prepare representative visuals and a timed boardmatic (a rough sequence of storyboard frames or placeholders with the accepted narration). Develop the palette, typography, image treatment, motion language and transitions together as an art direction Michael can judge. Use independent critics to check the visual evidence and consistency, and get Michael's direction approval before systematic asset scouting.

A boardmatic is the planning cut. It does not replace the finished first-minute V1.

SCOUTING

Hunt for video, stock footage, archival material, images and other assets against the narration beats and established visual DNA. Use saved project/Gallery assets and references where they fit. Show actual footage with chronological frames or playable clips so Michael and the critics can judge what it contains.

Saved YouTube competitor videos use the separate list_video_refs feature (for example, collection: "youtube-cars-competitors"); ordinary asset search does not return them. Keep these research records distinct from the production assets and technique skills filed in YOUTUBE CARS.

For each chosen asset, record its beat, source, useful moment, retrieval location and expected local path; record relevant usage rights or licensing information when available. Retrieve the chosen files and verify they can open. Mark coverage gaps rather than filling them with unrelated material.

Run independent critic agents alongside scouting. They inspect the actual material, cross-check what it shows against the words it would accompany, challenge weak choices and check coverage and visual consistency. The scout cannot certify its own selections. Repair mismatches and complete the timed boardmatic using the accepted assets, preserving the cohesion and picture-word alignment rule above.

V1 — FIRST MINUTE

Once scouting and preparation are complete, make the first production version cover the first 60 seconds end to end: accepted narration, finished visual treatment, footage and graphics, purposeful motion, editing, music, sound effects and their mix. Use this minute to prove the film's execution before extending production.

The first 30 seconds must be the strongest possible opening. Put the most effort into the immediate hook, clear promise, tension and forward movement. Make narration, pictures and sound support the same story moment. The critic reviews the complete minute with sound and separately assesses the first 30 seconds; fix the identified weaknesses and review the revised cut.

Show Michael the playable first-minute V1 with its critic findings and obtain his explicit approval. Do not proceed into producing the remaining minutes until he approves this first minute. This gate follows his script approval; it is a separate decision about the finished execution.

Continue the film

After first-minute approval, extend the established narration, visual DNA and editing treatment to the rest of the film. Keep independent critic checks during production. Store approved versions, critic findings, unresolved issues and asset retrieval information in the film's own documentation, keeping the shared rules free of film-progress notes.
